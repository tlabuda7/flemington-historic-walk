## Things to know before reading the entries

1. **No contributing / non-contributing status.** The 1980 form does not classify buildings.
   Every inventory entry, including ones the form itself calls "modern" (e.g. #26, the 1960 County
   Office Building), has the same hand-written tick in the right margin (Item 7, pp. 101–103,
   PDF pp. 73–75). The only different mark is an "X" beside "#60-66 Main Street – is part of #56"
   (p. 102). So the ticks are a checker's marks, not a status. Item 7 cont. (PDF p. 5) only says
   "Intrusions are less than ten percent of the total" of ~600 properties.
2. **The form describes 1979, not 1920.** Many of our 1921 buildings had been replaced or refaced
   by then.
3. **Street numbers are 1979 numbers** and do not always match the 1921 Sanborn numbers our specs use.
   The form is even inconsistent with itself: the store we call 78 is "#80" in the inventory
   (Item 7 p. 103) but "No. 78" in the significance text (Item 8 p. 4, PDF p. 9); the Union Hotel is
   "#70-74" (Item 7 p. 102) and "70 Main Street" (Item 8 p. 6, PDF p. 11). Matches below are made by
   order on the page, neighbours and description; confidence is given for each.
4. Inventory order for Main St in our area (Item 7 pp. 100–103, PDF pp. 72–75), as printed:
   #18-22, #24 | #26, #31, #32-38, #35, #37, #40-44, #50, #55, #56 | behind #56, #57, behind #57,
   #59, County Office Building (1960), #60-66, #68, #70-74 (Union Hotel), County Hall of Records |
   Courthouse, #79, #80, #82, #91, #93, #90-104, #110. **No entries** for 27, 28, 30, 41, 47–49,
   65, 84, 94–108 under those numbers.

## 24–30 Main (east side; 1921: 24 "D", 26 "Mill'y", 28 "Conf'y", 30 "Barber")

1921 Sanborn sheet 2 (checked for this note, px x 3950–4250, y 3600–4250): 20 "Gro." and 22 are
2-storey frame stores; **24** a 2-storey frame dwelling "D" with a front porch; **26** a 2-storey
frame "Mill'y" with a 1-storey front porch; **28 "Conf'y" and 30 "Barber"** one 2-storey frame block
of two units with a 2-storey rear part. The `bldg-24-30-main` placeholder spans about 26–30.

What the form says:
- **#24** (Item 7 p. 100, PDF p. 72): "a 2-story, early 20th Century structure"; stucco around the
  first-floor storefront; 2nd floor stucco walls "with small, rectangular windows"; asphalt-shingle
  **gable roof with modillions under the eaves**.
- **#26** (Item 7 p. 101, PDF p. 73): "a 1½-story, modern structure"; brick around the storefront;
  steep shake roof with inset dormers. Modern, so it **replaced** whatever stood there in 1921.
- **#28 / #30**: no inventory entry. But the significance text names "**No. 28 Main Street**" as a fine
  asymmetrical Italianate "L-shaped" house type "without the tower" (Item 8 p. 4, PDF p. 9).
- **#18-22** (Item 7 p. 100): 2-storey frame, mid-19th c., Italianate, L-shaped, low slate gable roof,
  big ornamental bargeboards, brackets, "a wide oriel window hangs from the 2nd-story end bay", 3-bay
  porch with brackets and cutout trim. (North of our block; matches 1921 20–22, which shows bays.)

Match / confidence: #24 = 1921 no. 24 dwelling "D", **medium** (same number, 2 storeys; but "early
20th Century" and stucco suggest it was rebuilt or refaced after 1921, so it says nothing firm about
1920). #26 = 1921 26 site, **medium**, modern replacement. "No. 28" Italianate L-house: **low**; the
1921 map shows 28–30 as one shop block, not an L-shaped villa, so either the form's "28" is another
numbering or a slip. Nothing here describes the 1920 fronts.

## 27 Main (west side; 1921: dry goods & clothing, old nos. 519 A/B)

**No #27 entry.** The west-side entries jump from **#15** ("located at the corner of Capner and Main
Streets… a one-story, brick, mid-20th Century structure in the 'Williamsburg' style", Item 7 p. 100)
to **#31** ("a 3-story, mid-20th Century, commercial structure with a flat roof and storefront on
the first floor", p. 101). Confidence **medium** that the 1921 building at 27 was gone (or swallowed
by a mid-20th-century building) by 1979. Nothing for the 1920 spec.

## 32–38 Main (east side; 1921 frame row "Mill'y", "Bakery", "Meats")

- **#32-38** (Item 7 p. 101, PDF p. 73): "a 2-story, 3-unit, brick, commercial structure built around
  the mid-20th Century."

Match: **high** (same number range, same three units, 2 storeys). By 1979 the 1921 frame row had
been **replaced (or entirely re-faced) in brick** in the mid-20th century. Nothing about the 1920
frame row. Our spec (frame, 2 storeys, 3 units) is not contradicted for 1920.

## 35–41 Main (west side; 1921 frame "Gro." 35–37, 3 storeys, and brick "Cobbler" 39–41, 2 + B)

- **#35** (Item 7 p. 101): "a 2-unit, brick, mid-20th Century structure. The southern half is 3
  stories high, and the northern unit is 1 story."
- **#37** (Item 7 p. 101): "a 2-story, brick, early 20th Century, commercial structure."
- No #39 or #41 entry.

Match: #37 (2-storey brick, early 20th c., south of #35) = 1921 brick "Cobbler" 39–41: **medium**
(storeys, material and position fit; the number does not). #35 (mid-20th-c. brick, 3-storey south
half) = 1921 frame "Gro." 35–37 site: **medium-low**; if right, the 3-storey frame store was rebuilt
or bricked over after 1921. The "early 20th Century" date for #37 fits a brick shop standing by
1921; it gives no detail of the front. **No contradiction** with the approved/built 35–41 spec.

## 57 Main (west side; 1921: 3-storey brick block north of the Wurts office)

- **#57** (Item 7 p. 102, PDF p. 74): "a 4-unit, 2-story, brick, commercial structure built in the
  early 20th Century with **striated brick walls** and a flat roof. The ground floor consists of
  aluminum storefronts."
- **Behind #57**: a 1-storey modern brick ranch-style building, and "a 2-story, 3-bay, early 20th
  Century structure with a gable roof, simple openings with wide frames, and a 3-bay, front porch
  with short, square supports on stone pedestals" (p. 102).

Match: **medium** (same number, next to #59 Wurts, which is certain). **Conflict to resolve when this
building is specced**: the 1921 map (per the Wurts spec, PLAN.md) shows a **3-storey** brick block
here; the form says **2-story, 4-unit**, with striated (scratch-face / tapestry) brick, which is a
1910s–20s brick type. Either the block was rebuilt or cut down between 1921 and 1979, or the 1921
"57" block is a different building. Re-measure the map (storey figure and height note) before
trusting either.

## 90–94 Main (east side; Masonic Hall / Hunterdon County National Bank)

- **#90-104** (Item 7 p. 103, PDF p. 75): "a 3-story, 5-bay, brick and stone structure built in the
  early 20th Century (as a **relica [sic] of Ford's Theater** in Washington, D.C.)." "Wide, gable roof
  with corbelled bricks, brackets, and arched wood trim under the eaves." "Each bay is articulated by
  brick pilasters and arched windows." "The first floor is faced with **rusticated stone** and has an
  entrance with **Neo-Classical details**."

Match: **high** (3 storeys, 5 bays, gable front, pilasters, arched windows, stone ground floor). The
form's "early 20th Century" date is **wrong** for the building (1864–66 is documented in our spec);
it probably reflects the 1921/1938 stone ground floor. The "Ford's Theater replica" is a local
tradition (both buildings date from the 1860s); not evidence of design intent. Everything else
**agrees with the approved spec**. New detail worth a look: "**arched wood trim under the eaves**"
and "**brackets**" along the gable, in addition to the corbelling (the spec has a corbelled raking
cornice; check the c. 1918 / 1938 photos for wooden arched trim under the rake).

## 96–98, 100–102, 106 Main (east side; 1921: 96–98 2-storey frame, 100–102 3-storey brick "Auto Supplies", 106 3-storey frame)

- **No entries under these numbers.** The Masonic Hall entry is numbered "#90-104", and the next
  entry is **#110** (Item 7 p. 103): "a 3-story, 3-unit, brick structure built in the early 20th
  Century. It has a flat roof and a cornice ornamented with brackets and modillions. Windows have
  segmentally-arched, brick hoods. The first floor facade has aluminum storefronts and stone treatment."

Match: **low**. "#90-104" is probably a lot/address range for the bank property, not proof that
96–106 had been absorbed into the hall. #110 (3-storey brick, segmental brick hoods, bracketed
modillion cornice) **could** be the 1921 brick 100–102 "Auto Supplies" (perhaps with 106 rebuilt
beside it as a third unit), but the numbers are 8–10 apart, so treat it as a lead, not a fact. If
used, it suggests for 100–102: segmental brick window hoods and a bracket + modillion cornice. The
two frame buildings (96–98, 106) are not described anywhere in the form.

## Other Main St notes from the significance text (Item 8)

- Item 8 p. 4 (PDF p. 9): symmetrical Italianate blocks "with an almost flat roof that has wide,
  overhanging eaves", often with "a low, segmental-arch pediment on the roof centered on the facade
  and supported by large, decorative brackets grouped in pairs" — examples: County Hall of Records,
  Nos. 78, 138, 179, 182 Main; with cupola: the Shields Building (52 Main), Beats Building (120–124).
- Item 8 p. 6 (PDF p. 11): the Union Hotel (70 Main), "built in 1876, is the most grandiose example"
  of Second Empire, "using a straight outline of mansard roof".
- Item 8 p. 7 (PDF p. 12): the early 20th century was "a period of languishing economy", reflected in
  smaller, simpler buildings (bungalows) — background for 1920.
