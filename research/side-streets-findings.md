## 1. Fire Dept House, 7 Court St (K1) — still PLAIN-GUESS front, history known

**Found.**
- [Snell] 1881 and [GS-Hopewell]: in 1862 citizens bought a steam fire engine and built "a commodious
  two-story engine-house ... on the lot adjoining and west of the court-house, on Lyceum Street"
  (Lyceum St = today's Court St). The upper floor was an **assembly room** for the fire company.
  [HCHS-gems] (1902 Sanborn): "2 floor brick engine house" on the west side of the jail yard.
  [BK1898]: "The town furnishes a good house for all apparatus and a room for the meetings".
- [HCHS-trail] stop 29: the borough **bought the old Lyceum / Reading Academy at 38 Park Ave in 1922**
  for a Borough Hall and Fire Department. So in Sept 1921 the company was still in the 1862 building
  on Court St (agrees with the map's "Fire Dept House"); it moved out the next year.
- The **"FIRE HOUSE, FLEMINGTON, N.J." postcard** on [GS-Hopewell] (image
  https://goodspeedhistories.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Firehouse-copy.jpg ) shows a 2-storey
  stuccoed building, pedimented front gable with a round date plaque, a recessed upper loggia on two
  square piers, two folding glazed engine doors under "FLEMINGTON FIRE DEPT", a 1920s pumper inside,
  and a sidewalk along its long left side. **This is almost certainly the converted Lyceum at
  Court St / Park Ave after 1922, not the 7 Court St house**: the 1980 NRHP describes Borough Hall at
  Court and Park as "2-story, early 20th Century, stuccoed structure with a pedimented gable roof and
  4 garage doors facing the Park Avenue side"; the 1921 map gives 7 Court St a frame lean-to on the
  west side and the jail-yard wall on the east, neither of which is in the card. Do not use it for K1.
- **The "hose tower" is withdrawn.** Re-checking `flmngtncountycrthousvu09.jpg`: the camera stands
  north-east of the courthouse (79 Main is on the left = south), so the slim capped tower rises
  beyond the courthouse's **north** flank, i.e. north-west of the building. The fire house is
  south-west, behind the courthouse from that viewpoint. The tower is something on the courthouse /
  jail lot (probably a ventilating or boiler stack), not the fire house.
- 1982 HCHS image "Flemington Fire Department 1842–1982, 140th anniversary" booklet cover
  (https://hunterdonhistory.org/flemington-fire-dept/): four small photos of apparatus; no
  identifiable view of the Court St house at web size.

**Form / materials.** 2-storey brick (map + 1902 + 1881 text), 1862 build, apparatus doors below and a
meeting room above. Front layout, roof form and doors: **no image**. Confidence: history high,
front **low (guess)**.

## 2. Opera House, Bloomfield Ave (B5) — PLAIN-GUESS, but its 1921 state is known

**Found.**
- [S21-4] (Sept 1921, plate 4, top left) draws the building with the label **"OPERA HO. — Damaged By
  Fire"**, 2 storeys, frame, a 1-storey rear annex; the plate-1 index lists "Opera House ... 2".
  So in Sept 1921 it **stood but was fire-damaged** (and not then the town's picture house; the
  Palace Theatre had taken over).
- [FJCC]: the Jewish congregation founded in 1922 held services "at the Flemington Opera House" in
  its first years, so it was **repaired and usable again soon after**.
- [CT]: built 1878 (credited to Peter Emery, for a Presbyterian Sunday School; unverified), 500 seats
  (1897–98 Cahn guide), stage 20 ft deep, proscenium 22 × 10 ft; later lecture hall, school, movie
  house, fur factory, house; **demolished 1965** for a parking lot. A photo is held by the Hunterdon
  County Library (North Branch) local history room (not online).
- No photo found online.

**Form.** Map only (2-storey frame hall, 30 ft front part, 20 ft rear part, gable assumed along the
length, front to the south). Confidence of form: **low**. For Sept 1921: intact-looking shell is
acceptable at 110 m; optional plain boarded windows to hint at the fire (owner decision).

## 3a. Methodist Episcopal Church, 116 Main at Maple Ave (E7) — PHOTO-BASED

**Found.**
- **[BK1910] page n38, "(3) Methodist"** (saved: `booklets/booklet1910-methodist-church.jpg`):
  view from the south-west street corner. Rock-faced **grey stone** in regular courses; two equal
  **steep gables**, one facing Main St and one facing Maple Ave, each with one **large pointed-arch
  traceried window**; a **square corner tower** in the angle between them, with a pointed-arch
  entrance at its foot, a small pointed window above, a belfry stage with **two tall louvred lancets
  per face**, a corbelled band, **four corner pinnacles** and a steep **pyramidal spire** about as
  tall again as the belfry stage, small cross at the tip; a lower hipped part on the far right
  (rear / chapel); a low basement band with small windows; small cross finials on the gable apexes.
- [BK1898] "Methodist" (saved: `booklets/booklet1898-churches.jpg`): same church, gable-end to the
  street with the big pointed window, tower to its right.
- [BK1910] text: lot at Main and Maple bought 1883, cornerstone 4 Sept 1886, dedicated 22 Feb 1888,
  **54 × 120 ft, "built of gray stone"**; [BK1898]: "similar in style to the Presbyterian church,
  though not so large, ... commodious chapel in the rear".
- [NRHP] 1980: Gothic Revival, stone, steeply-pitched intersecting slate gable roofs, 2-storey tower
  with the entrance vestibule at the corner, a large pointed-arch stained-glass window centred on
  each of the two street facades. [HCHS-trail] stop 15 (1886). Still standing; modern photos show
  the same form.
- Map (sheet 3, already measured): belfry "35'" at the SW corner, second tower "24'", walls 18'.

**Form.** Cross-gabled stone church, tower in the SW (Main / Maple) corner. 35 ft = top of the tower
masonry; the spire rises about 7–9 m more (photo estimate; tip ≈ 18 m). Slate roofs dark grey.
Confidence **high**.

## 3b. Presbyterian Church, 10 East Main (head of Main St) — PHOTO-BASED, now mapped

**Found.**
- **[S21-5]** (Sept 1921 plate 5, Commons) draws it: **stone**, "16'–20' to eaves", **"SPIRE 110'"**
  at the **south-west corner**, front (south) block about 28–31 m wide × 14 m, rear chapel block
  with canted ends to about 41 m total depth. Registered to the scene through the church-lot corner
  that also appears on sheet 2 (sheet-5 px (1290, 5740) = scene (2.9, −332.3); sheet 5 is 100 ft/in,
  1 px = 0.1033 m): **front wall z ≈ −357 (SW) … −352 (SE, the wall is slightly skewed), x ≈ 4.4 …
  34.9; rear to z ≈ −406; spire at about (7.5, −368) ±3 m.** The monument triangle is on sheet 2 at
  (8.2, −312.6) (re-checked).
- [BK1910] text: dedicated 1 Nov 1883; **138 ft deep × 90 ft broad**; audience room 75 × 90, chapel
  63 × 90. [BK1898]: "beautiful Gothic structure of **dark gray stone**". [HCHS-gems] 1902 Sanborn:
  "60 ft steeple on its southwest corner" (1921 sheet says 110').
- Photos: `flmngtnmainstnorth13.jpg` (local, 1913); **[BK1910] n38 "(2) Presbyterian"**
  (`booklets/booklet1910-presbyterian-church.jpg`, from the south-east: steep main gable, chapel with
  hipped roofs and a big stone chimney, ivy); **[BK1898] "Presbyterian"** (front from the south: low
  front range with a row of round-headed windows and a gabled porch, spire on the left);
  [FPC] c. 1910 card (front: tall square tower with a slender spire on the left, main gable with a
  round rose window, a lower front range with five small round-arched windows and a gabled entrance
  porch). [NRHP]: steep slate gable roof with small gables on the south side, "tall tower with a
  steep pyramid roof". Still standing.

**Form.** Dark grey stone; square SW tower with a tall slender spire to 110 ft (33.5 m); steep main
gable with rose window behind a lower front range; chapel behind. Confidence **high** for form and
position (±5 m).

## 3c. Soldiers' Monument (north end of Main St) — PHOTO-BASED

**Found.** [WM]: commissioned 1891, dedicated 30 May 1892 by GAR Post 48; sculptor E. B. Koch;
statue of a Union soldier in **Westerly granite** on a **Barre granite** base over mortared stone;
battle names on the lower base. [NRHP]: stone, about **20 ft** high (6 m). Photos: [BK1898] "Main and
Broad Streets" (`booklets/booklet1898-main-and-broad-monument.jpg`): **pale grey-white** stepped base,
die, tall pedestal, standing soldier; a **low iron railing** round the triangular grass plot with a
lamp or pump post at its tip; [BK1910] "(4) Soldiers Monument" (`booklets/booklet1910-soldiers-monument.jpg`,
c. 1909: plot edged by a low stone or hedge border); local `flmngtnmainstnorth13.jpg`. The cannon
and the flagpole now beside it are later (Spanish-American War gun per HMDB snippet; leave out).
Position: sheet 2 triangle, statue at (8.2, −312.6). Confidence **high**.

- **American Legion room, Steam Laundry, the brick garage, the "Boarding" stable, Mallory machine
  shop**: no photo found. The 1910 booklet has only a product advert for the Mallory Manufacturing
  Co. ("Mallory shutter worker"), no building.
- **Higgins Ave (Union) = today's Chorister Place** ([HCHS-gems]: "Union (now Chorister Place)").
  **H1 "Auto 1st, Hall 2d (stuccoed)" at 3 Higgins is the Flemington Children's Choir School
  building** ([HCHS-trail] stop 13: 3 Chorister Place, built as a stable, choir school 1895–1958).
  Still standing; no period photo found.
- **Court St park and band stand**: [BK1910] "(2) Public Park" (`booklets/booklet1910-public-park.jpg`,
  c. 1909, taken from an upper window): curving gravel paths, lawns with many young shrubs and

  raised floor and a pointed (ogee-ish) roof, and a 2½-storey house with a bracketed cornice
  beyond. Confirms the band stand and gives the park's look (the park dates from 1901–02,
  [HCHS-trail] stop 30).
- **Lyceum / Reading Academy, 38 Park Ave** (Court St / Park Ave corner, the far end of the Court St
  view): in 1921 still the Lyceum hall (bought by the borough only in 1922). The post-1922 card
  above gives its form (2 storeys, pedimented front gable, upper loggia) but its engine doors are
  from the 1922 conversion. It is off sheets 2 and 3; position not measured.

## 5. Street ends

- **E2 12–16 Main**: [NRHP] #14 Main "3-story, early 20th c., with a 2-story porch on simple square
  columns" (matches the map's 3-storey frame with 2-storey porch). Medium.
- **E3/E4 18–22 Main**: [NRHP] "#18-22 Main: 2-story, frame, mid-19th c., **Italianate**, L-shaped,
  low-pitched slate gable roof, large ornamental bargeboards and brackets, a wide **oriel** hanging
  from the 2nd-storey end bay, a 3-bay porch with brackets and cutout trim". Medium (one building
  in 1980; the 1921 map splits it into 18 Boarding and 20–22 Gro.).
- **E1 Capner corner block**: replaced (1980 "#15 Main: one-story, brick, mid-20th c."). No photo;
  map only. Low.
- **E5 108–112 Main**: [NRHP] "#110 Main: 3-story, 3-unit brick, early 20th c., flat roof, cornice
  with brackets and modillions, segmental-arched brick window hoods". Medium.
- **E6 114 Main is the Doric House** (1846, Mahlon Fisher; HCHS museum, still standing):
  [NRHP] 3-bay, 2½-storey frame Greek Revival, flat-roofed front with a portico, wide entablature
  with small frieze windows with iron grilles, Doric columns ([NRHP] p.8: "Doric columns of the
  Doric House"), classical doorway with transom; beside it a 1-storey Greek Revival frame office with
  laurel wreaths, dentils and pilasters; cast-iron fence on the street line. Modern photos exist
  on hunterdonhistory.org. Medium-high (unchanged building). Should not be a plain "dwelling".
- **E7 Methodist church** and **Presbyterian church / monument**: see 3a–3c.
- `flmngntnmainstreetsouthfrmonument08.jpg` (local, 1908): looking south from the monument; large
  frame houses with porches and big trees on both sides, no commercial buildings in the first
  ~100 m. Useful only as general character for whatever closes the view north of Capner St.
